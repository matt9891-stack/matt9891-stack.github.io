Summary Post – Industry 4.0 and Industry 5.0 in Retail

The whole discussion highlighted how the digitalisation of retail could bring both opportunities and, on the other hand, organisational vulnerability. The initial post highlighted how the M&S cyber incident in 2025 can show how third parties, in a more digitalised landscape, can expose retailers to significant operational and financial risks.
The discussion extended this view beyond the technical point as Damiano highlighted the importance of considering the M&S incident also from a socio-technical point of view. He also highlighted the tension between digital efficiency and operational safety, as precautionary shutdowns can protect an organisation while simultaneously reducing its ability to operate normally. 
In this direction, Ali's post suggests that resilience in Industry 5.0 should focus not only on preventing attacks, but also on the ability to detect, respond to and recover from disruption while maintaining essential operations.
AlHidaifi, Asghar and Ansari (2024) distinguish cyber resilience from traditional cybersecurity by highlighting organisations’ capacity to absorb, recover and adapt to cyber incidents. 
Similarly, Gaudenzi and Baldi (2024) highlight the importance of cyber resilience also across organisational and supply-chain relationships, which is particularly relevant to retailers that might depend on interconnected third-party services.
John also developed this point by distinguishing prevention from operational resilience, by highlighting that supplier assurance, restricted access and secure credential procedures can reduce the likelihood and impact of an attack, without ensuring that a disruption will be prevented. 
Overall, the discussion suggests that the transition from Industry 4.0 towards Industry 5.0 should not be deployed merely as a digitalisation replacement, but it should also involve security measures and risk management. 
An Effective retail cybersecurity framework should then involve a combination of preventative controls, resilient system design, human-centred processes and business continuity planning. 
The M&S case demonstrates that the objective should not simply be to prevent every attack but also ensure that when a disruption happens, the organisation can maintain essential services, protect customers and recover their operations safely.
